- **33 degrees of BONEYARD membership** (Scottish Rite echo — needs the real degree structure researched for the FINISHED file). Degree 1: pressed the paw. Degree 3: found the bone in the letterform — a Master Drafter; most stop here, like real lodges. Higher degrees honorary: drew a house in an actual 5 minutes, spotted a trap street in the DRAFT font, survived a client's spouse changing the kitchen 11 times. The 33rd is by invitation of the dog only; nobody knows what the 33rd degree gets you. Newbie degrees earn their keep with honest shitwork first, apprentice-style — the Entered Apprentice carried the stones before he cut them: redline a messy plan, trace an underlay, dimension by hand before AUTO DIMS is revealed to them. Perks (the good shelf, member marks, the vocabulary, DOG MODE) unlock by degree. All of it voluntary — nobody assigned it, they asked for it, which is why they'll value it. Earned status binds (founder's own army experience: once you gain status it's hard to leave — it becomes identity, not subscription): degrees must be earned, never bought or gifted, and the door out stays as open as the door in — free will both directions, which is exactly what makes staying mean something.
- **The degrees are a drafting education in disguise.** They come for the 5-minute house; the climb quietly teaches real craft — why dimension strings stack the way they do, how a footing carries a wall, lettering and lineweight discipline, measure-true ethics, the history chapters. Like the lodges: ritual was the delivery mechanism, teaching was the point — apprentices came for the trade and left with principles. Nobody signs up for a drafting course, but everyone wants the next degree.
- **The ritual IS the teaching — the experience is the lesson.** Design rule for every BONEYARD ceremony: never explain the principle, make them live it. The candidate at the lodge door wasn't told free will matters — he affirmed it standing there. Ours: voluntary discovery is learned by pressing the paw, earned status by doing the apprentice work, humility by the dog outranking you. Deliver the principle through what happens to them, not what's read to them — you can forget what you read, not what happened to you.
- **Membership makes you like a dog — the knowing look.** Dogs have that look in their eye like they know a good secret and aren't telling: content, loyal, faintly amused. That's the face of a BONEYARD member watching a coworker fight AutoCAD for three hours — says nothing, sips his coffee, little glint. The secret doesn't make you talk, it makes you wag. But trustworthy — the dog is the original trustworthy one: knows everything about the household, tells nobody, keeps the secret not because he's sworn to but because he'd never betray you. That's what separates the knowing look from a smirk. The RUFF drafter, defined.
