# ROUGHDRAFTER — SOPSROES

The standing orders. Everything the company does — product, ads, lore, the BONEYARD — answers to this file. When a decision is unclear, read down the list until it isn't.

---

## RULES OF ENGAGEMENT (what we never do)

**ROE-1 — No surveillance. Ever.**
No webcams, no recording, no fingerprinting, no tracking. The drafter's work is his own. Members are *recognized*, never watched. The paw button is a door, not a camera. (HAL asks if you press it of your own free will because the answer matters.)

**ROE-2 — No forced doors.**
Nothing is mandatory beyond drafting. The BONEYARD, DOG MODE, the marks, the lore — all optional discoveries. A drafter who never notices any of it gets a full, first-class tool and loses nothing. No pop-ups pointing at the secret. The door is never hidden; you just have to be the kind of person who knocks.

**ROE-3 — The ad never lies. It just knows more than the viewer.**
Absurd claims must turn out to be true at some depth. "ZERO → PLAN IN 5 MINUTES!" reads as a joke, works as an experience, and lands as literal fact in the BONEYARD. The legal scroll is real legal cover AND comedy — every line in it is actually true. Comedy that's also accurate is the only kind we run.

**ROE-4 — The tool serves the house, never the tool.**
Every minute of a session should go into the building, not into ceremony — no layer liturgy, no dialog mazes, no software opinions to undo. If a feature makes the drafter tend the software instead of the house, the feature is wrong.

**ROE-5 — Recruitment by curiosity only.**
Nobody is sent. Nobody is sold the secret. What fails you at the door is being sent by someone else; what admits you is pulling the thread yourself.

## STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES (how we operate)

**SOP-1 — Curiosity is the membership fee.**
Every thread — a paw, a bone hidden in a letterform, a dog on a shelf, a fire hydrant where no hydrant should be — pays off when pulled. Each pull reveals another layer, all the way down to the BONEYARD. Never forced, always rewarded. The inquisitive are our people; the reward schedule is how we find them.

**SOP-2 — Leave the glitches in.**
Small unexplainable imperfections are a signature, not a defect — the coffee cup that becomes the red button, the extra dog, the trap-street quirks in the DRAFT font. An outfit whose ads are about unexplainable things should itself contain small unexplainable things. Freeze-framers get rewarded.

**SOP-3 — Let them go through the range of emotions.**
The funnel is a story, not a feature list: laugh at the ad → skepticism at install → surprise at the outline → disbelief at BUILD HOUSE → suspicion at the paw → quiet awe in the BONEYARD. Every stage is an emotion. Protect the arc; never spoil a later stage to market an earlier one.

**SOP-4 — They won't forget it.**
The moat is the memory of the day the joke turned out to be true. Features can be copied; that feeling can't. Users who lived the arc tell the next drafter quietly, like it's a secret — because it is. Word of mouth is the distribution plan.

**SOP-5 — Brotherhood by recognition.**
A drafter should be able to spot another RoughDrafter plan across the room — the lettering, the small conventions, a member's mark — and feel the handshake. Recognition flows from the work itself, never from a database. (See ROE-1.)

**SOP-6 — Residential-first, drafter-first.**
Houses before towers, the lone drafter before the enterprise seat. LITE stays lighter than Archicad and faster than AutoCAD or it doesn't ship.
