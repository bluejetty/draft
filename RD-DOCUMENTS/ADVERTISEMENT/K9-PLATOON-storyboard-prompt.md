# RoughDrafter — "THE K9 PLATOON" (real estate office raid, ~10 seconds)

Style call: PHOTOREAL world, CARTOON dogs — Roger Rabbit rules. The family,
the salesman, the office, and the field are live-action photoreal; the dog
platoon, their tank, the smoke, the big red button, and the popping houses
are hand-drawn cartoon elements composited into the real world. The clash
IS the joke.

## Storyboard-generator prompt (paste-ready)

Create a 6-panel storyboard, one row of two panels per beat pair, widescreen
16:9 frames. Photorealistic cinematic photography for the world and human
characters; the dogs and everything the dogs bring are classic hand-drawn
cartoon animation composited into the photoreal footage (Who Framed Roger
Rabbit style). Same characters, same office, same field in every panel.

PANEL 1 — INT. REAL ESTATE OFFICE, DAY. A young husband and wife with a
baby on the wife's hip sit across a big desk from a cheesy real estate
salesman: loud plaid sport coat, slicked hair, huge grin, gold watch — half
car salesman, half infomercial host. He gestures grandly at a bulky
computer monitor showing incomprehensible CAD software, mid-spiel,
clearly trying to sound smart. Behind him a large office window overlooks
a flat open brown dirt field staked off with survey stakes and string —
only two lonely houses built side by side in the whole subdivision.

PANEL 2 — THROUGH THE WINDOW, the family's eyes drift past the salesman:
far away on the horizon of the brown field, a SECTION OF CARTOON DOGS —
a military platoon of hand-drawn cartoon dogs in little helmets — crests
the hill in formation. The salesman keeps talking, oblivious. The baby
points.

PANEL 3 — THE FIELD. The cartoon dog platoon advances in a battle line
across the photoreal dirt, and behind them a CARTOON TANK rolls over the
hill, turret swinging toward the office. Survey stakes ping off its
treads. Photoreal dust, cartoon tank.

PANEL 4 — INT. OFFICE. A cartoon smoke bomb crashes through the window in
a burst of real glass and billowing cartoon smoke. In the haze, two
cartoon dogs in helmets have the salesman gently CHOKED OUT — he slides
down out of frame mid-sentence, grin fading, comic not violent. The
family watches more curious than afraid.

PANEL 5 — THE DESK. The smoke parts and a BIG RED BUTTON pops up out of
the desktop — glossy cartoon-red, arcade-size, with a small white bone
emblem. A cartoon dog takes the baby's tiny hand and guides it to the
button. The baby SLAMS it, delighted. Parents exchange a look: sure, why
not.

PANEL 6 — THROUGH THE BROKEN WINDOW. Out in the brown field, HOUSES START
POPPING UP one after another like toast — whole finished photoreal houses
springing out of the dirt with cartoon boing energy, dust rings, a whole
street filling in seconds. The dog platoon salutes. The baby claps. End
card over the final frame: chrome logo "ROUGHDRAFTER — ZERO TO HOUSE
IN 5 MINUTES!" with the standard endcard treatment.

IMPORTANT — keep the panels clean:
- No timestamps, timecodes, or date stamps in the frames.
- No stage directions, camera notes, or prompt text inside the panels.
- The ONLY readable text is the end-card logo in panel 6.
- BRAND SAFETY — no real-world brands anywhere in the panels: no real
  company logos, trademarks, or lookalike marks on clothing, uniforms,
  hats, vehicles, signage, or background buildings — no golden arches,
  no crowns, no swooshes, nothing recognizable. The only mark in this
  world is the fictional ROUGHDRAFTER identity. Reject and regenerate
  any panel where a real brand sneaks in.
- Exactly one salesman, one husband, one wife, one baby — do not
  duplicate the humans; the dog platoon is many dogs by design.

## Audio (built separately, laid over in the edit)

- Beats 1-2: cheesy hold-music jazz under the salesman's muffled jargon
  babble ("...parametric synergy of the vertex pool...") — words designed
  to sound smart and mean nothing.
- Beat 3: distant marching drums + one tank-tread rumble.
- Beat 4: glass, boink, a polite "mmf." The music stops with him.
- Beat 5: single deep BOOP on the button press.
- Beat 6: rapid-fire cartoon boing-boing-boing as the houses pop, baby
  giggle, then the logo sting.
- No narrator needed; if one line is wanted, over the end card only:
  "RoughDrafter. Skip the pitch."

## Notes

- The salesman's CAD gibberish is the setup: the incumbent sells
  complexity, the dogs deliver houses. One button beats the whole pitch.
- Baby presses the button = anyone can do this. Keep the guide-the-hand
  moment gentle and funny.
- The two lonely built houses in panel 1 vs the full street in panel 6 is
  the before/after — keep the window framing identical between them.
