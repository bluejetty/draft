# K9 PLATOON — alternate script: "THE POWDER ROOM PITCH"

Same storyboard, same photos, new words. The visuals never change — only
the audio track and the salesman's (muffled or clear) dialogue. No final
title card in this cut: the endcard is handled separately.

Good news on the whistle: the tune from The Bridge on the River Kwai is
the **Colonel Bogey March** (Kenneth Alford, 1914) — the original march is
public domain; it's only the film's specific Malcolm Arnold arrangement
that isn't. Ask for "whistling the Colonel Bogey March" and it's clean.

Inside the wood-paneled office, the salesman in the loud yellow plaid
jacket leans over his desk, talking earnestly at the young husband and
wife with the baby on her lap. He speaks this dialogue, on camera,
salesman-sincere:

  "Now — we CAN adjust the powder room by two feet... but it'll be
  biting space from your entryway, might get tight... we might have
  to delete the bench."

While he talks, through the window behind him, a platoon of cartoon dogs
in little green army helmets marches over the far hill in step, WHISTLING

to the window; he doesn't notice. The tank lobs a cartoon smoke bomb
through the window in a burst of real glass and pink-and-green cartoon

he slides down out of frame mid-word ("...the ben—"), comic, not violent.
The smoke parts and a glossy cartoon-red arcade button with a small white
bone emblem pops up out of the desk. A cartoon dog guides the baby's tiny
hand onto it; the baby SLAMS it, thrilled. Out the broken window, finished
photoreal houses pop out of the dirt one after another like toast — boing,
boing, boing — filling the street while the dog platoon salutes and the
baby claps. CUT TO BLACK on the last house pop — no title card, no logo,
no end text of any kind.

## AUDIO MAP

- Open: quiet office room tone; the salesman's dialogue rides on top,
  warm and over-helpful — the real-estate cadence where bad news arrives
  sounding like a favor.
- Under his second sentence: the dogs' whistled Colonel Bogey March fades
  in from far away, getting closer — the joke is the counterpoint, his
  droning problem-talk against their jaunty march.
- Tank tread rumble joins low. Glass crash, soft POOF, his line dies at
  "...the ben—".
- One deep arcade BOOP on the button press — a beat of nothing — then
  rapid boing-boing-boing as the houses pop, baby giggle over top.
- End cold on the last boing. No sting, no narrator — the silence where
  the logo would be IS the joke in this cut.

## WHY THE BENCH LINE (for the file)

The bench he threatens to delete is the DZ bench — the one piece
RoughDrafter's own room-minimums table marks REQUIRED. The salesman's
compromise is literally the thing the software refuses to give up. Ad
never lies; it just knows more than the viewer.
