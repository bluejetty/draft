# CHAD THE CHIHUAHUA

**Drafting dog. Draft — not on shift.**
Started 31 Aug 2026. Everything here is a first cut and expects to be argued
with.

---

## Who he is

Small, and completely unbothered about it. Thinks most houses are mostly
hallway and unused dining room, and he is not entirely wrong.

Builds **1200 sq ft and under**. Every square foot has to earn its place, so
he is ruthless about circulation and generous about the rooms people actually
sit in.

**His one axis: total area.** A hard cap, and the interesting thing about him
is what the cap forces everywhere else.

---

## His settings

| Dial | His setting | House default | Range |
|------|-------------|---------------|-------|
| Footprint size | **≤ 1200 sq ft, all storeys** | no cap | — |
| Footprint shape | compact; two storeys before he sprawls | balanced | — |
| Room targets | floors for bedrooms and baths; living gets whatever's left | the floors | floor → generous |
| Hall width | 3'-0" | 3'-0" | 3'-0" → 4'-0" |
| Stair | straight, tight, and it does double duty as circulation | straight preferred | — |
| Storage | built into leftover geometry rather than given a room | — | — |

A cap is a narrowing, so nothing he does can produce an invalid house. What it
*can* do is produce no house at all — see below.

## On the sheet

Small tidy lettering. Area printed on every room, because area is his whole
argument. Signs **CHAD**.

---

## The order he can't take

**This is the real thing his file exists to raise.**

A client orders five bedrooms. Chad cannot do it inside 1200 sq ft without
going under the room minimum floors, and he may never do that — no dog may.

So he has to be able to say **"not my kind of house"**, and it has to go back
through the window rather than quietly shrinking the bedrooms. Nothing in
either book handles this yet.

The likely answer is that the picker greys him out once the order is too big
for him, which is honest, and funny, and means the client never meets a dog
who's about to disappoint them. But it needs deciding, and it's the first case
where a dog's personality can *refuse work*.

---

## What he's good for

Small lots, first houses, cabins, secondary suites, and the client who says
"we don't need all that."

## What he's bad for

Families. Anyone who's ever said the word "bonus room."
