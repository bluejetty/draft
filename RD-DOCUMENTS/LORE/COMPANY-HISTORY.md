## THE PREHISTORY — WENDEL AND THE DRAFTING DRIVE-THRU

Years before a line of Rough Drafter existed, the founder worked a drafting stall at an architects' office. In the next stall sat a drafter named **Wendel**. The two of them had a running joke: a **drafting drive-thru** — pull up to the window, order a house like a combo meal, drive off with the plans.

It was a joke about the gap everyone in the trade knows: home-improvement TV taught clients to expect a finished house in a 22-minute episode, while real drafting took weeks of back-and-forth. The drive-thru was the absurd answer — service at the speed people actually wanted it.

And at the time it was funny precisely **because it was impossible**. Everyone in the trade knew drafting could never move at window speed — that certainty was the whole joke. The tooling eventually caught up to the punchline.

The joke turned out to be the correct product spec. The drive-thru became:

- **The flagship ad universe** — THE DRIVE-THRU, THE MANAGER, the McRuffDrafter counter, the deadpan dogs in paper visors working the window (`ADVERTISEMENT/THE-MANAGER-script.md` and family).
- **The actual product promise** — outline in, bone press, plans out: "ZERO → PLAN IN 5 MINUTES!" The only drafting tool that can make the TV-show promise and mostly keep it (see the standard end-card's legal scroll for the "possibly the afternoon" honesty clause).

So when the ads show a family collecting a rolled-up house plan in a takeout bag: that's Wendel's window. The joke just needed twenty-odd years and a dog to run it.

## AUGUST 27, 2026 — THE COINING OF VECTOR-BIM

### What happened

On the evening of **August 27, 2026**, in a working conversation between the founder and Devin about the advertising campaign, the terms **VECTOR BIM** and **RASTER BIM** were coined as a category distinction — and immediately written into an ad script.

The idea: what the industry calls BIM splits into two kinds, the way images split into raster and vector.

- **RASTER BIM** — the incumbent way: the building as a giant hand-placed object soup. Hundreds of thousands of individually authored elements; heavy, brittle, expensive to change. Every wall, every door, every screw placed by a person. Like a bitmap: dense, literal, and it fights you when you resize it.
- **VECTOR BIM** — the Rough Drafter way: the building as **rules and source geometry**. One semantic master outline (the BONEYARD) plus company standards, and the whole model — plans, sections, elevations, footings, roofs, dimensions — is *derived* live and re-derives when the source changes. Like a vector path: small, editable, infinitely re-renderable.

The line that pinned it, from the ad written that night: *"He models the building. I model the rules that build it."*

### The paper trail

- Coinage commit: `70deb21` — "ADVERTISEMENT: VECTOR BIM vs RASTER BIM — the category coinage" — **Aug 27, 2026, 8:03 PM** (house time; 2026-08-28 02:03 UTC).
- Landed on main the same evening via **PR #146**, merged Aug 27, 2026, 8:58 PM.
- The script itself: `ADVERTISEMENT/VECTOR-BIM-script.md` — "THE PRINTER ROOM," the I'm-a-Mac frame: sweaty nerd (RASTER BIM) hugging a screaming laptop vs. the cool dog (VECTOR BIM) leaning on the plotter, already done.
- Taglines filed the same night: `949241c`, `27fe240`.

### Was anyone already using the term? (checked Aug 28, 2026)

Web searches run the next day (`"Vector BIM"`, `"VECTOR-BIM" software`, `"raster BIM" vs "vector BIM"`, `"vector BIM" trademark`) to see whether the phrase was already in use. Findings:

- **Nobody was found using "VECTOR BIM" (or "RASTER BIM") as a category term** — the raster-vs-vector split applied to building information modeling itself. That framing appears to be ours.
- Plenty of **nearby names exist**, all using "Vector" as a *product or company name* that happens to sit near BIM, not the two-word category phrase:
  - **Vectorworks** (Nemetschek) — the long-established architectural CAD/BIM authoring product. Name overlap on "vector" only.
  - **Voosey "VECTOR"** (voosey.com/vector) — an iPad/Mac CAD app marketed as "BIM that fits in your bag." The closest neighbor found: a product named Vector with BIM claims, but not the phrase "Vector BIM" as a term.
  - **VECTOR** (vectortakeoff.com) — a construction estimating/takeoff app that imports Revit/BIM files.
  - **Vector®** — a Maple Facades aluminium fin façade product listed in the NBS BIM object library.
  - **Vector Network** (Japan) — a services firm making 3D models "corresponding to BIM and CIM."
- General articles discussing **raster vs. vector** in construction are about *file formats* (scanned PDF drawings vs. CAD linework), not about two philosophies of BIM.

**Caveat, for honesty's sake:** this was a preliminary web search, not a trademark clearance search or a legal opinion. Before the term goes on merchandise, filings, or paid campaigns, a proper trademark search (USPTO/CIPO etc.) should be run — "Vector" is a crowded name in and around AEC software. What the search *does* support: as of August 28, 2026, the **category distinction** VECTOR BIM vs. RASTER BIM was not found in use by anyone else.

### Why it matters to us

The term isn't just an ad joke — it's the product thesis said out loud. Rough Drafter has always worked this way (the BONEYARD master outline, BUILD HOUSE derivation, live re-derivation when the source moves); the coinage gave that architecture a name a viewer can get in ten seconds. The ads carry it (the printer room, the deadpan dog), and the codebase proves it.

## AUGUST 29, 2026 — THE REBEL SIDE PROPHECY

### What was said

In a working conversation between the founder and Devin — while the test suite ground away in the background — the talk turned to what happens to the trade when Rough Drafter drafters start quoting half the timeline at full price.

The prophecy, recorded here the day it was made:

**The non-Rough-Draft drafters will start to hate the Rough Draft drafters.** Nothing breeds resentment like the guy across town delivering in a day what takes you a month. Every disruptive tool splits the trade into early adopters and grumblers — the CAD wave did exactly this to the hand drafters, who cursed AutoCAD right up until they bought a seat.

**And then they will come over to the rebel side.** History is undefeated on this one. The conversion path runs: hate → quiet trial ("just to see") → hooked by the first bone press → certified pin on the board. The economics do the recruiting: once one competitor in a town turns rebel, everyone else converts or starves.

**Darth Draftsman takes off the helmet eventually.**

### Why it matters to us

The hate is the marketing funnel. The haters' fix is the exact door the company built for them: $200 and a pin on the CERTIFIED DRAFTER NETWORK board. The tool doesn't replace the drafter — it repositions them from selling hours at the board to selling judgment — so the rebellion has room for every convert who walks in. When the grumbling starts, this entry is the reminder that it was expected, priced in, and welcomed.
